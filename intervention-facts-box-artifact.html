Formulas & references

RR = risk ratio, OR = odds ratio, MD = mean difference, p₀ = baseline (control) risk, U/L = upper/lower 95% CI bound, Z = 1.95996 (97.5th percentile of the standard normal).

Relative risk reduction (RRR)
RRR = 1 − RR  ·  CI: 1 − RR₍U₎ to 1 − RR₍L₎
Absolute risk reduction (ARR) & treatment risk
ARR = p₀ × (1 − RR),   treatment risk = p₀ × RR. Baseline risk is treated as fixed, so the ARR interval is p₀ × the RR interval.3
Number needed to treat (NNT)
NNT = 1 / ARR. When the ARR interval spans no effect, the CI is shown discontinuously as NNTB … ∞ … NNTH.1,2
Odds ratio → risk ratio
RR = OR / (1 − p₀ + p₀ × OR), evaluated at the baseline risk; the RRR/ARR/NNT above then follow.4
Mean difference (MD) — relative change
relative change = MD / baseline mean × 100% (CI bounds divided by the same baseline mean).
95% confidence interval
Ratios (RR/OR), on the log scale: exp(ln θ ± Z × SE_log), with SE_log = [ln(U) − ln(L)] / (2Z). Differences (MD): θ ± Z × SE, SE = (U − L) / (2Z).5
Sampling-distribution curve
Ratios: ln θ ~ Normal(ln θ̂, SE_log²) — drawn log-normal on the ratio axis (density φ(ln θ)/θ) or Normal on the log axis. Differences: Normal(θ̂, SE²) on a linear axis. Normal density φ(x) = exp(−½((x−μ)/σ)²) / (σ√(2π)).5
